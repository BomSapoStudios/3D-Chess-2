1. OVERVIEW
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

3D Chess 2 plays chess inside a cube. Pieces move along X, Y and Z at once,
each player starts on their own face of the cube, and armies meet in the
middle. Games are 2, 4 or 6 players, on the phone (against the computer or
pass-and-play) or online, with optional Chaos Mode spins and chess clocks.
There is also a single-player Zombie Survival mode.

Board Sizes (shown in the menu as meal sizes):
  • Snack 5³  — 5x5x5 — 125 cells — 2 players only
  • Meal 6³   — 6x6x6 — 216 cells — 2 or 4 players
  • Feast 8³  — 8x8x8 — 512 cells — 2, 4 or 6 players (6 players always 8³)
  (4x4x4 was the 2-player board until 2026-10-25. It was replaced because the
  two pawn walls started touching and White won nearly every game. 4x4x4 is
  now only used by Queen Blitz puzzles built on it.)

Player Colors (in turn order):
  White, Black, Green, Purple, Yellow, Orange
  2 players: White, Black
  4 players: White, Black, Green, Purple
  6 players: all six

2. THE 3D BOARD
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Each cell has a 3-coordinate position (X, Y, Z).

  • X-axis — left to right
  • Y-axis — bottom to top (the floors / levels)
  • Z-axis — front to back

Squares are named A1a-style: file letter, floor number, rank letter.

Each player owns one face of the cube and advances toward the opposite face:

  Color          Advance Axis   Starting Face
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────
  White          +X             X = 0
  Black          -X             X = max
  Green          +Z             Z = 0
  Purple         -Z             Z = max
  Yellow         +Y             Y = 0   (bottom)
  Orange         -Y             Y = max (top)

Drag to orbit the cube, pinch to zoom. The game opens looking from your own
side. View buttons and Level buttons are covered in section 11.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
3. PIECES & MOVEMENT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Pieces capture by landing on an enemy piece. Only Knights jump; every other
piece is blocked by anything in its path. No move may leave your own King
in check.

KING
  1 cell in any direction: up to 26 neighbouring cells in 3D.

QUEEN
  Any distance in a straight line along an axis (like a Rook) or any
  diagonal, including the 3D corner-to-corner diagonals (like a Bishop).

ROOK
  Any distance along one axis: left/right, up/down, forward/back.

BISHOP
  Any distance diagonally: 2-axis diagonals within a plane and true 3D
  diagonals moving along all three axes at once.

KNIGHT
  An L-shape: 2 cells along one axis, then 1 along a perpendicular axis.
  Jumps over pieces. Up to 24 targets in 3D.

PAWN
  Move:     1 cell forward along your advance axis, onto an empty cell.
            No double step, no moving backward, no capturing straight ahead.
  Capture:  any of the 8 cells one step forward and one step sideways
            (the ring of cells around the cell straight ahead).
  Promote:  on reaching the far face of the cube, choose Queen, Rook,
            Bishop or Knight. The computer picks for itself.
  No en passant and no castling.

Piece values used by the computer and the Turn Planner:
  Pawn 1, Knight 3, Bishop 3.2, Rook 5, Queen 9.

4. SETUP: PLACING YOUR PIECES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Every game starts with a placement phase. You design your own opening.

Your bench (every board size):
  1 King, 1 Queen, 2 Rooks, 2 Bishops, 2 Knights   (8 pieces)

Rules:
  • Every piece must be placed before Ready can be pressed.
  • Pieces go on your own starting face, within your placement zone:
      5³  — your whole face (25 squares); pawns spawn on the next layer,
            leaving an empty middle layer between the two armies
      6³  — part of your face (White/Black: Y 3–5, Z 1–4;
                               Green/Purple: Y 0–2, X 1–4)
      8³  — the middle 4x4 of your face
    so nobody starts within reach of anyone else.
  • Everyone places at the same time. Robots place automatically.
  • When play starts, a Pawn appears directly in front of each placed piece
    (one step along your advance axis), giving every player 8 Pawns.
  • Play starts once every player is Ready.

Changing your mind:
  • Tap a placed piece, then tap another square in your zone to move it.
  • Or tap it and press "Back to bench" to take it off the board.
  • Both work online too. Once you press Ready your setup is locked.
  • Online, setup is private: you only see your own pieces until every
    player is Ready, then everyone's pieces appear and play starts.
  • Floors outside your zone are dimmed while you place.

Who moves first:
  • Normally White.
  • If anyone starts the game in check (any player count: e.g. White's
    setup leaves Black's King attacked), the first player in check (in turn
    order) moves first, and no King may be captured until
    everyone who started in check has had a turn to get out of it.

5. CHECK, CHECKMATE & HOW GAMES END
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

CHECK
  Your King is attacked by at least one enemy piece. You must get out of it
  on your move (move the King, block, or capture the attacker). With several
  players you can be checked by more than one opponent at once.

CHECKMATE
  In check with no legal move that escapes it.
    2 players: the game ends; the checkmating player wins.
    4/6 players: the checkmated player is out (see Conquest, section 6).

STALEMATE
  Not in check but no legal move.
    2 players: draw.
    4/6 players: that player just sits out the turn. If nobody can move at
    all, the game is a draw.

OTHER DRAWS
  • Insufficient material — nobody has enough left to checkmate.
  • Threefold repetition — the same position occurs three times.
  • Draw by agreement — see Offer Draw below.

OTHER WAYS TO LOSE
  • Forfeit — you resign; you lose immediately.
  • Clock — your chess clock or 24-hour move window runs out (section 8).
  • Walking out of an online game — counts as a loss for rankings.

OFFER DRAW
  • Only on your own turn. Your opponent can accept or decline.
  • Works online and on the phone (the computer decides for itself).
  • Not available in Zombie Survival.

The result appears in a neon banner, followed by the Rematch screen.

6. MULTI-PLAYER RULES (4 AND 6 PLAYERS)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Board size: 4 players need Meal 6³ or Feast 8³; 6 players always Feast 8³.

Turn order: White → Black → Green → Purple → Yellow → Orange, skipping
anyone who is out (or stalemated that turn).

CONQUEST
  When you checkmate a player, their King is removed and all of their other
  surviving pieces become yours, where they stand.

FORFEITING WITH SEVERAL PLAYERS
  • If you forfeit while in check, it counts as being checkmated by the
    player who checked you first: they win your pieces.
  • Otherwise your pieces are simply removed and the game goes on.

VICTORY
  The last player with a King wins.

7. CHAOS MODE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

"Up, down, sideways. Go wild." Every few turns part of the cube spins like
a Rubik's cube, carrying the pieces on it.

  Interval: Every 5, Every 9 or Every 15 turns.

  Spin types:
    Face      — an outer face of the cube turns
    Layer     — an inner slice turns
    Diagonal  — a turn around a diagonal axis

  • The spin is animated; clocks pause while it plays.
  • Undo can't go back past a spin.
  • Plan ahead: a safe square may not stay safe.

8. CLOCKS & PACE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

CHESS CLOCK (any game; "No clock" by default)
  • 3, 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes per player.
  • Only the player on turn's clock runs; it pauses for animations,
    Chaos spins and promotion.
  • A warning shows under 1 minute.
  • Running out loses the game. With more than two players left, the game
    ends with no winner.

ONLINE PACE
  Speed round (live)
    • Everyone plays now. Optional chess clock as above.
    • A player who drops has 5 minutes to come back before their seat is
      given up.
    • If nobody places or moves for 30 minutes, the game ends.
  Take your time ("Up to a day per move")
    • No chess clock; each move (and the placement phase) may take up to
      24 hours. Running out of time loses.
    • You can leave the app between moves; a push tells you when it's your
      turn, and another about 2 hours before your time runs out.

9. THE COMPUTER OPPONENT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

"Robot brain size" — three difficulties:

  Pea      (Easy)    Looks 1 move ahead, with random noise and the occasional
                     deliberate blunder.
  Walnut   (Medium)  Searches 4 moves deep, about 1.2 seconds per move.
  Galaxy   (Hard)    Searches up to 8 moves deep, about 3 seconds per move.

How it thinks:
  • Alpha-beta search on a fast internal board, run on a background thread
    so the game never freezes.
  • Iterative deepening, a transposition table and killer moves.
  • Weighs material, central pieces, pieces attacked and defended,
    mobility, and the pieces around each King.
  • Chooses its own promotions, places its own pieces in setup and answers
    draw offers.
  • Any seat in a local game can be swapped between You and Robot. Online,
    any seat but the host's can be a Robot, as long as one is left for a
    human opponent.

10. ZOMBIE SURVIVAL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A single-player survival mode on the "Zombies!" button. Hold off a horde of
zombie pawns for as long as you can.

Setup:
  • Feast 8³ board. You are White: King, Queen, 2 Rooks, 2 Bishops,
    2 Knights and no pawns.
  • You may place anywhere on your whole first plane (X = 0).
  • Choose Pea, Walnut or Galaxy.

The horde:
  • One zombie appears at the start, then one more each round on the far
    face. From round 25 (Pea), 15 (Walnut) or 10 (Galaxy), two a round.
  • Zombies march toward you (along −X), one step a round, front-most first.
  • A zombie bites any of your pieces on its forward diagonals; if it
    can't move forward it waits.
  • A zombie that reaches your side promotes: to a Knight on Pea, a Queen
    on Walnut and Galaxy. Promoted zombies hunt your pieces.
  • Galaxy only: a piece that gets bitten rises again as a zombie.

Rules that differ from chess:
  • There is no check. Your King is just another piece.
  • The run ends when your last piece falls.
  • No draw offers.

Score:
  10 per round survived, 5 per zombie pawn destroyed, 20 per promoted zombie
  destroyed. Your best score is kept separately for each difficulty.

Pawns:
  Every zombie you destroy (pawn or promoted) earns pawns for your pawn bank: 1 on Pea,
  1.5 on Walnut, 2 on Galaxy, with the run's total rounded down. Up to 100 pawns a day.
  Undo can't earn the same zombie twice. See PAWNS below.

Zombie Hunt (the Turn Planner in this mode):
  For each zombie, the fastest way one of your pieces can destroy it within
  4 moves, aiming at where the zombie will be rather than where it is.
  The hunter's route shows in cyan, the zombie's predicted path in pink and
  the kill square in lime. Routes that never stop where a zombie could bite
  are preferred, and the cheaper piece is sent when two are equally fast.

10b. QUEEN BLITZ
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A single-player puzzle mode on the "Queen Blitz" button. Clear every pawn with a lone Queen
in as few moves as possible.

Moves:
  • The Queen slides any distance along any of her 26 lines and knocks out every pawn she
    passes through, not just the one she stops on. Pawns never move.
  • Blocks can't be entered or passed; they stop her on the square before them.
  • Ice floor: she can't stop partway. Every move slides to the board's edge or the square
    before a block.

Placing the Queen:
  • Early puzzles place her for you. Later ones let you put her on any free square
    (placing isn't a move). Dots only appear once you pick her up.
  • "Auto-place" puts her on a square that can reach par, for 1 pawn (greyed out with none).

Scoring:
  • Par is the true fewest moves, found by the solver in Tools/QueenBlitz (with free
    placement when you place the Queen). The goal is to match it.
  • The first time you clear a puzzle you keep all of its pawns (the puzzle card shows
    what each one pays). Replays pay nothing; the card then shows your best moves vs par.
  • Undo, Restart, Retry and Next puzzle.

PAWNS
  Pawns are what you collect, for a future store (piece sets, puzzle packs). They live in
  a pawn bank on the phone. Queen Blitz pays each puzzle's pawns on its first clear;
  Zombie Survival pays 1 / 1.5 / 2 a zombie (Pea / Walnut / Galaxy), up to 100 a day;
  Auto-place costs 1. Store prices (not built yet): piece set 4,500, puzzle pack 3,000.
  The numbers are all in Assets/Scripts/PawnBank.cs.

Screen:
  • The puzzle name, moves, par and pawns left sit at the very top, with the puzzle's tip
    under them. Turn Planner and the turn line are hidden; How to Play shows only these rules.

The free Starter pack (ten puzzles): 1–4 and 8 give the Queen; 5–7, 9 and 10 let you place
her; blocks appear from 6 (Roadblock: one block on the corner of an L turns 2 moves into 3);
8–10 are ice (pars 1, 2, 2, 3, 2, 3, 3, 2, 4, 5).

Puzzle files:
  • Each pack is a JSON file in Assets/Resources/QueenBlitz (starter.json so far). Each puzzle
    lists its pawns, blocks, queen (empty = you place her), par, an Auto-place start and one
    par solution. Tools/QueenBlitz/packs.py re-solves every pack and checks them;
    puzzles.py --export rewrites starter.json.

11. IN-GAME TOOLS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

TURN PLANNER
  Steps through each of your pieces and each of its moves, best first, with
  squares named A1a-style. For the chosen move it shows what it captures,
  what could take the piece on its new square, which of your pieces it
  leaves open, and what it threatens. While open, the board is stripped down
  to the pieces involved with coloured markers and lines. Tapping one of
  your pieces on the board plans that piece.

UNDO
  • Puts the board back to the start of your previous turn (against the
    computer, that takes back your move and its reply). Up to 30 turns.
  • Available in local games, Quick Play and Zombie Survival.
  • Not available online, with a chess clock running, or past a Chaos spin
    or a player being knocked out.

SAVED GAMES (RESUME)
  • Quick Play, Zombies!, Queen Blitz and Local Game each keep one saved
    game on the phone. It is saved at the start of every turn you play:
    every piece and square, whose turn it is, the chess clocks (kept current
    through the turn), the count to the next Chaos spin, players knocked out,
    the zombie round and score, and the puzzle's move count.
  • Go back to the home screen at any time (to change Customize or
    Accessibility settings, for example). The in-game menu's button reads
    "Main Menu (game saved)".
  • Tapping that mode again shows GAME IN PROGRESS with a one-line summary
    ("Playing Black on Snack 5³, 12 moves in", "Walnut run, round 4,
    score 85", "Puzzle 3, 2 moves so far"). Resume skips setup and puts the
    saved position back with your current settings. Start new opens the
    mode as usual; the save is replaced once the new game starts.
  • A finished game (checkmate, draw, forfeit, a zombie run ending, a
    cleared puzzle) deletes its save. Online games aren't saved here (the
    server keeps them).
  • Undo history and the move list start fresh after Resume.

VIEW BUTTONS (bottom of the screen)
  Swing the camera behind any player's pieces, about 25° up, framing the
  whole cube. Yellow looks up from underneath; Orange looks down from above.

LEVEL BUTTONS (right edge)
  Show a single floor of the cube, hiding the others so the inside can be
  seen and tapped. "All" shows everything again.

OFFER DRAW / FORFEIT / HOME
  Offer Draw and Forfeit ask for confirmation first (see section 5).

ON-BOARD FEEDBACK
  • Legal destinations light up when a piece is selected.
  • A King in check, and the pieces checking it, are highlighted with a
    check status message.
  • Moves, captures and Chaos spins are animated, with haptics.
  • Promotions play the animation chosen in Customize.

12. ONLINE PLAY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The server runs on Cloudflare (Workers + Durable Objects) and checks every
rule itself: placement zones, the full bench, legal moves, turn order,
promotion, draws and clocks.

ACCOUNT
  Sign in with Apple. You get a random online name (e.g. shinyrook359),
  which you can change, and a friend code.

PLAY ANYONE — Find Match
  • Choose board size and player count; you're matched with others who
    asked for the same. Nobody waits in the queue more than 5 minutes.
  • Always ranked: a 10-minute clock each, no Chaos Mode.
  • Paused for a while if you keep walking out of games (section 14).

PLAY A FRIEND
  • Create a room and share its code, or challenge a friend directly
    (they get a push and an Accept / Decline).
  • Friends: find people by name or code, send and accept requests.
  • Room seats can be filled with Robots.

ROOMS
  • A live lobby closes after 10 minutes if nobody joins; "Extend" adds
    10 minutes, up to an hour. A Take your time lobby stays open a day.
  • A lobby closes 2 minutes after its host leaves.
  • Finished games close a few minutes after they end.
  • "Your games" lists games in progress to resume.

13. REMATCH
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

ONLINE
  • After the result, a 15-second countdown asks everyone for a rematch.
  • It happens only if every human still there says yes, and at least two
    humans are left.
  • The rematch is a new private room with everyone's seats reserved. Human
    seats rotate so someone else plays White. Anyone who left is replaced
    by a Robot.
  • Otherwise everyone goes home.

ON THE PHONE
  Rematch restarts the same game with the same settings, no countdown.

14. RANKINGS & STATS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Ranked online games against other people are rated (Elo, starting at 1000).

Only Find Match games are ranked: random opponents, a 10-minute clock each and
no Chaos Mode, so friends can't trade rating points. Games with friends (a room
code, a challenge or a rematch) are unranked: they can use Chaos Mode or no
clock, and still count in your win/loss record and head-to-head, but don't
change ratings.

Two ladders:
  Duel   — 2-player games.
  Melee  — 4- and 6-player games, scored pairwise by finishing order
           (each player is compared with every other player).

Rating changes:
  • K = 40 for your first 10 games, then 20.
  • The same two players only move each other's ratings in their first
    5 games together each day.

Tiers:
  Pawn    under 950, or fewer than 5 games played
  Knight  950+
  Bishop  1100+
  Rook    1250+
  Queen   1400+
  King    1600+

Walking out:
  • Leaving a game early counts as a loss.
  • A third walk-out within 24 hours pauses Find Match for 15 minutes; each
    further one doubles the pause, up to 4 hours. Resets after 7 days.
    Games with friends still work.

My stats (Stats screen):
  Rating and tier for each ladder, wins / losses / draws, pieces taken and
  lost, recent games, and a leaderboard (friends or everyone, Duel or
  Melee). Games against the computer are tracked separately on the phone,
  by difficulty, along with Zombie Survival best scores.

15. NOTIFICATIONS

Push notifications, each switchable on the Notifications screen:
  • It's my move
  • Someone joins my game
  • My game is ready (it started)
  • A friend challenges me
  • Friend requests (new and accepted)
  • Take your time games: a warning about 2 hours before your 24 hours run out

Pushes go only to players who aren't currently in the game.

15b. ACCESSIBILITY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The Accessibility button on the home screen (next to Notifications) opens a
screen of switches. Every visual option starts OFF, and none of them follows
the iPhone's own settings: the game looks exactly as usual until a player turns
one on here. Choices are kept on the phone (PlayerPrefs, keys A11y.*).

  Reduce Motion        — fades and still frames in place of big movement.
                         Home screen buttons stop rocking and rippling (the
                         blob shape holds still). Chaos spins: each piece fades
                         out and back in at its new square. Promotions always
                         use the short Fade animation. Captured pieces, Queen
                         Blitz pawns and a conquered king fade where they stand
                         (no flying shards, knocked-flying pawns or orbs).
                         Pieces glide straight instead of hopping; no landing
                         bounce, swell or spreading rings. View buttons cut
                         straight to the new view. Check and threat bubbles stop
                         pulsing. Backdrops (Melting Clocks, Deep Sea, Earth
                         Orbit, Neon Jungle, Cyber City, Graveyard, Neon Desert,
                         Deep Space) and their particles slow to nearly still.
                         Gameplay timing is unchanged: a fade takes as long as
                         the move it replaces.
  Color-Blind Symbols  — each player has a shape as well as a color:
                           White ● circle     Black ▲ triangle   Green ■ square
                           Purple ◆ diamond   Yellow ★ star      Orange ✚ cross
                         The shape floats over every piece (board and benches)
                         and shows on the on-screen bench, Ready buttons, View
                         buttons, the turn banner, the chess clocks, the online
                         "You are…" line and the result panel. A king in check
                         gets a warning sign and each piece giving check a
                         crosshair, so check doesn't rely on red alone. Green
                         turns teal, Yellow paler and Orange deeper, so those
                         three differ in lightness too. Queen Blitz: pawns taken
                         this run get a tick in the tracker.
  High Contrast        — aims for 4.5:1 for small text. Dark plates behind small
                         neon text (menus and labels over the board), a darker
                         turn banner, lighter "unavailable" purple, solid
                         placement dots, more solid selection, capture and check
                         bubbles, and during setup your usable squares brighten
                         while the rest go much darker.
  Larger Text          — 1.5x or 2x for the menus (home, setup, Customize and its
                         store panel, online screens, Notifications,
                         Accessibility, the pop-up pickers), the How to Play
                         guide and the result panel. Screens that no longer fit
                         scroll (card screens restack into one column); the guide
                         becomes one scrolling column. Text during play is not
                         scaled yet.
  Haptics (ON)         — taps and buzzes on iPhone, on by default with an off
                         switch: button taps, picking up and placing pieces,
                         moves landing, captures, zombie bites, check (warning),
                         promotions and wins (success), losses and overruns
                         (error), your turn coming back to you, and a light tick
                         per pawn a Queen Blitz sweep knocks out (bunched up so a
                         long sweep doesn't buzz nonstop).
